Turbulent flow through a 90° bend

Does a hexmesh bend mesh, with a standard turbulence model, reproduce how a 90° bend pushes the fastest flow against the outer wall, as laser measurements at PTB Berlin show it?

Passes both gates
  • At level 2 (450 k cells) the velocity profiles are within 0.060–0.096 UbThe bulk velocity: the flow rate divided by the pipe's area. 0.40 m/s here. of the measurements at all six planes. The gateA pass/fail limit written down before any run. A case that misses is reported as a miss, not tuned until it passes. is 0.10.
  • The fastest flow sits on the outer wall at every plane, as measured.
  • The bend's loss coefficient K is 0.262; Ito's correlation gives 0.263. Reported, not gated.

How sure: two mesh levels, so no GCIGrid convergence index: an error band estimated from three or more mesh levels. With two levels it can't be computed. yet. K moved 19 % between the levels, so the close match to Ito at level 2 may be partly luck. Level 3 (1.5 M cells) is open to volunteers.

The setup

Geometry

Water enters from below, turns right through the bend and leaves 42 D later. The last 10 D (shaded) have cells that grow toward the outlet, to keep the outlet away from the measurements. D = 0.1 m.

Boundary conditions, in words

BoundaryWhat happens there
inletFully developed turbulent pipe flow at Ub = 0.40 m/s, computed first in a short periodic pipe on the same mesh section.
wallNo slip. The first cell is 50 viscous unitsThe near-wall length scale ν/uτ. A first-cell centre at y⁺ 20–30 suits wall functions. thick at every level, so the wall model stays the same as the mesh is refined.
outletPressure 0. Backflow, if any, enters at the inside values.
fluidWater at 30 °C, ν = 8.0 × 10⁻⁷ m²/s. Re = 50 000.

Mesh section, level 1

Drawn from the level's parameters: 32 cells around, 8 across the ring, a square core. Level 2 doubles them, keeping the wall cell.

Reference and gates

Laser-Doppler measurements of the axial velocity along the horizontal line through the axis, at six planes after the bend (Straka et al. 2020, as Ekat et al. 2023 Fig. 3 report them). Gates, judged at the finest level: at each plane the RMS difference is within 0.10 Ub, and the peak is on the outer wall.

Run it

meshhexmesh only → mesh files, mesh.json
setupproblem.json → OpenFOAM case
runresumable chunks, stop rule
extract→ samples.csv, a slice
evaluate→ quantities, gates
plot→ this sheet

Blue-topped stages are the same for every solver; brown-topped ones are OpenFOAM's. Another solver replaces the brown ones and hands its samples to evaluate.

Here, with OpenFOAM

python3 -m validation run turbulent_bend --level 1 --np 4
python3 -m validation run turbulent_bend --stage plot

Level 1: about 7 min on 4 cores. Level 2: about 5.3 h.

In your own solver

python3 -m validation bundle turbulent_bend --level 2
# mesh (.msh .unv .su2 .cgns), problem.json,
# lda.csv, samples layout
python3 -m validation compare turbulent_bend my_samples.csv

You get the same verdict table and these plots with your curve added.
